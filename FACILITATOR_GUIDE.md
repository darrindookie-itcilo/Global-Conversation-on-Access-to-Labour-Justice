# Facilitator guide

## Build a Bridge: introduction exercise
Allow 20–30 minutes; for a large room, invite a selection of groups to present.

1. Open Facilitator studio and sign in with your configured facilitator credentials.
2. Set target group size (2 for pairs) and create a room. Open the projector and show it on the main screen.
3. Participants scan the QR code or open Live room and enter the displayed code. Each joins with a preferred name, country/institution and professional perspective. No participant account is needed for this exercise.
4. Once participants have joined, click Balance groups. Read out the groups and ask people to sit together. An odd number can produce a trio. Increase target size to form larger groups; manual moves accommodate late arrivals.
5. Give groups 6–8 minutes: introduce yourselves, agree on one shared access-to-justice challenge and one question for the week. Each person records their own perspective and experience, plus the agreed challenge and question.
6. Each participant clicks Share with the room. Group cards and shared counts update on the projector approximately every 2.5 seconds. Private drafts are not projected.
7. Click Present / pause editing, then Spotlight this group. Give each invited group 60–90 seconds to introduce its members and explain its challenge and question. Show all groups restores the full wall.
8. Export shared results, close the room when finished, and invite participants to open My notebook to record one useful connection or question. Saving the personal notebook requires a workshop account; downloadable worksheets offer a paper alternative.

## Suggested session placement
| Session | Game | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday opening plenary | Build a Bridge live icebreaker | 20–30 min |
| Monday understanding disputes / psychology | Missing Voice, or a Case Challenge | 10–20 min |
| Tuesday prevention, mediation or grievance handling | Case Challenge: repeated grievances / settlement | 5–10 min |
| Wednesday Track A evaluating institutions | JusticeWorks: choose reforms within a budget, explain trade-offs | 30–45 min |
| Wednesday Track B digitalising institutions | Digital Justice: test a service against different user needs | 20–30 min |
| Thursday platform economy / AI and inclusive access | Digital Justice; use Missing Voice for inclusion discussion | 20–30 min |
| Friday professional competencies / recap | Routes to Labour Justice in learning mode | 20–35 min |

Coordinate use with the session presenter. All games can also be used independently at a break or in a facilitated recap.

## Routes to Labour Justice
Open Workshop games, choose Routes, select 2–8 teams and choose opening or learning mode. Project the board. The facilitator rolls; the active team discusses the displayed prompt before the facilitator records the outcome. Opening mode gives each team three introduction turns. Learning mode uses fictional cases with explanations. Use Pause, +30 seconds, Undo and Finish & debrief as needed. Board movement contains chance; learning points are separate. This is a facilitator-operated whole-room game, not phone-to-phone multiplayer. The game saves on the browser/device used to run it.

## What works between sessions
Participants can explore themes, read resources, solve short cases, use session notebook prompts and maintain an action plan without presenter involvement. The live icebreaker needs an active room and connectivity. The personal notebook is private; only deliberately shared icebreaker contributions appear to the room.

## Notebook accounts and forgotten passwords
Open Facilitator studio → Participant notebook accounts. Enter a name and unique username, then choose Create notebook account. Give the displayed temporary password privately to the participant. They open My notebook, sign in, and choose a new password. To recover access, find their name and choose Reset password. All previous workshop sessions are revoked, but notes are preserved. Only the new temporary password is shown; their chosen password is never stored in readable form.

## Sharing and mobile
Share the live dashboard URL. Its page includes a title, description and the supplied course social card for Teams and WhatsApp previews. Those apps can cache old previews and may suppress previews under their own settings. Mobile navigation uses the menu button; tap outside it to close.

## Platform economy — Session 1: DOES IT SURVIVE?
Open Workshop games → DOES IT SURVIVE? → Facilitator. Sign in with the existing facilitator credentials, create a room and open the projector in a separate window. Teams enter the four-character room code and a team name, using one device per table. They retain their identity on that browser.

For every gate: Open gate → teams choose an option and type one reason → Close gate → Reveal → optionally award 0–5 judge points for each reason → Next gate. The 90-second clock is soft: answers stay open until you close them. After revealing gate 3 (or later), use DROP THE TWIST, discuss the new facts, then Continue after twist. Gate 6 is added exactly once. Zero-point answers stop the case marker, but the team continues playing. At the end, collect the one-sentence reflections and discuss the gate chart and the four grouped breakpoints (Access/forum, Procedure/evidence, Remedy, Enforcement). These four groupings are facilitation categories, not legal classifications.

## Platform economy — Session 2: RATIFY!
Keep the morning room. After ending Game 1, press Carry scores into RATIFY! once. Teams on the same browser automatically retain their names and balances; a new room starts without carried scores.

1. Start Article Sprint. Open each gap, allow article claims, then Close gap → Reveal → Next gap. A wrong claim costs 2 points and locks that team out for 5 seconds. At gap 10, use Start Model Market.
2. Teams receive one problem card and lock a model choice. Set a speaking team to start its 90-second timer. Open voting; other teams vote yes/not yet. Close voting to reveal the anonymous total and award model-match and vote points. Repeat for each team, then Start Green Challenge.
3. Teams submit gap rows. A GREEN row earns 12 points. Another team can challenge it once; each team has at most two challenges. Allow 60 seconds for the response, then choose UPHELD or OVERTURNED. Resolve all pending challenges before End game.
4. Export gap rows CSV before leaving. The UTF-8 CSV opens in Excel and supplies the rows for 30 November–4 December. Rooms are available for 24 hours.

Live games use the dashboard’s existing shared service, as selected by the course owner, rather than Firebase. Facilitator controls require server-validated authentication; participants use room codes only. All submitted text is displayed as text, and exports neutralize spreadsheet formulas.

If the connection fails, keep the current page open. Manual fallback creates a clearly labelled local scoreboard and does not overwrite live results. Run discussion aloud and adjust scores manually. To return to live play, reopen the original room code. Do not expect offline edits to synchronize.

## Balance the daily tracks
1. In Facilitator studio, create one workshop account per participant, or reuse their existing notebook account. Do not create a second account for a different device.
2. Under Daily allocation, choose Tuesday and confirm the people attending. Confirm all workshop accounts only after checking for test or duplicate accounts. For Wednesday and Thursday you can use Tuesday’s participant list, then adjust attendance.
3. Ask participants to open My programme, sign in and choose A, B or Either for each day. Each preference covers morning and afternoon together. The same account works on another device.
4. Review the live totals and ask for volunteers if one track is oversubscribed. Volunteers change their preference to Either while collection is open. Confirm that everyone has had that opportunity. Missing preferences must be collected before the draw.
5. Click Create balanced proposal. It closes preferences and uses a recorded random order to resolve oversubscribed choices. Review names and both totals. Manually move people if necessary, recording a reason.
6. Confirm and publish allocations. A split differing by more than one requires your explicit exception reason. Participants see their confirmed daily track in My programme, and can filter to their confirmed sessions.
7. Export the daily list as CSV. To change a published allocation, reopen that day with a reason. This withdraws its published assignments; participants see preferences open again until you confirm a replacement. Other days and icebreaker groups are unaffected.

Only confirmed attendance counts toward the target. A preference is not a booking. Participants cannot see other people’s names, choices or draft allocations. The recorded draw and history are available in the facilitator panel.


## Live games control centre
Open Facilitator studio and sign in. Live games is the first section. All seven workshop games have a Create room button; existing rooms show their teams, progress and scores. Select Manage room to use controls, export results, open the projector or delete the room. Platform rooms keep their existing scoring and controls, embedded here, and still expire for play after 24 hours. Retained expired results can be exported or deleted.

For JusticeWorks, The Missing Voice, Digital Justice and Case Challenge Library, teams open Join live room (or your room link), enter the code and a unique team name, and use one device per team. Activity selections/reveals report progress; teams explicitly submit their written response. Award 0–10 points with a short explanation. Revised submissions clear the previous award. Pause/finish blocks submissions on the server; reveal controls whether other teams/projector can see responses. Practice activities remain available without a live room.

Routes to Labour Justice uses the shared board inside Manage room. Set up 2–8 named teams on the facilitator device. Board names, positions, points and prompts synchronize to the room projector; phones are optional. Board controls remain facilitator-only. The board roster is the set of names entered during board setup, rather than the optional phone sign-in roster.

Delete room permanently removes its submissions and scores; export first. Confirmation is required. Icebreaker room deletion is next to the Existing room selector. It deletes all participants and cards for that room only.

Icebreaker participants can choose Exit session & return home, or Restart my icebreaker. Both remove their current member record, shared/private card and local identity; restart opens a fresh join form for the same room. Other participants and notebook accounts are unaffected. No action is taken on real rooms during deployment.


### Reset daily allocations
Under Keep both tracks balanced, expand Reset allocation / start again. Reset the selected day or all three days. By default, confirmed participant lists are kept; untick the checkbox to clear attendance too. Reset clears preferences, proposals and published assignments, reopens preference collection, and retains a reset entry in allocation history. Accounts, passwords, notebooks, icebreaker rooms and games are unaffected. Export each day before resetting if you need a copy. Reopen preferences is the lighter option: it preserves saved preferences and attendance but withdraws assignments so you can make another proposal.


## Platform game reliability update
Team submissions now use the team identity even if the device is also signed in as facilitator. Host controls still require facilitator sign-in. Remembered rooms are separate for each game. When a host carries scores into RATIFY!, connected Session 1 screens move to the matching Session 2 screen with the same team identity. A Session 1 room cannot be joined from the RATIFY! join form.

Polling runs every 2.5 seconds while visible, pauses after three consecutive failures and stops for a deleted or expired room. Reconnect retries explicitly; returning to the tab or regaining connectivity also resumes. Change room returns to the join form without deleting the previous team identity. Gap rows have server-side duplicate protection. Gate reasons remain required, as in the existing activity; the content and scoring values are unchanged.

Manual fallback uses an inline team-name form and a persistent local-only banner. Return to online room restores the live connection without sending manual scores. Manual scoreboards are saved separately for the two games. Project the current screen in manual mode; separate devices do not synchronize. Closed gate counters stop at the closing timestamp. Projector typography is enlarged and answer buttons include their A–D letters.

My action plan is now a dedicated five-field commitment card: breakpoint, concrete change, agreement, deadline and evidence of progress. It uses the same private workshop account, autosaves and exports to Word or print/PDF. Earlier action-plan notes remain available and are included in the export.
